The person behind Sorted.

Hey, I'm Adan Salahuddin.

Student. Applicant. Tired of guessing.

I built Sorted because applying to universities abroad often felt like trying to solve a puzzle without all the pieces. After surveying Pakistani students going through the same process, I realized we were all asking the same questions and searching through far too many tabs to find the answers.

So I built the resource I wish I'd had from day one. A place for opportunities, student friendly guides, research guidance, financial aid resources, O and A Level materials, and everything else that makes student life a little less confusing. It's still growing because students' questions keep evolving, and so will Sorted.

PHILOSOPHY • ECONOMICS

The veil you never lift

Why is this friend of mine so overly sensitive? God, it’s so hard to be around her.” “Why does this person always try to join our group? Get a life, dude.” “Why does this beggar keep asking for money when we’ve already refused politely?” “Bro should really update his fashion sense.” “Why is he doing O and A Levels privately? Has he suddenly become obsessed with studying and forgotten how to have fun?” “Why is he going to that university? He had decent grades. He probably just missed the deadlines.” We’ve all had thoughts like these. They feel harmless, even reasonable. The problem is that they’re all built on one assumption: that we know the whole story. But what if we simply don’t? What if the person with the “bad fashion sense” is wearing his late brother’s clothes because it’s the closest thing he has left to him? What if the student taking O and A Levels privately isn’t chasing perfect grades, but simply couldn’t afford college? What if the student who “missed the deadlines” was grieving the loss of a parent and university applications suddenly became the least important thing in his life? We spend so much time judging people’s choices that we forget we never lived the lives that produced them. Maybe that's where economics and philosophy quietly meet. One reminds us that our choices are shaped by the opportunities and limitations around us. The other asks an uncomfortable question: if none of us chose our starting point, how much of where we end up is really ours alone? Maybe that’s the veil we never leave. We look at outcomes and convince ourselves they reflect someone’s character, when they may simply reflect a reality we’ve never had to experience ourselves. Think about the beggar you walked past today. If you had been born into the same family, faced the same hardships, made the same impossible choices, and lived every day exactly as they did, are you so certain you wouldn’t have ended up in the same place? Maybe even worse. The uncomfortable truth is that none of us can answer that question honestly. That’s why I think judgment deserves more caution than certainty. Not because people are never responsible for their actions, but because we rarely know enough to decide how much of those actions were choice, and how much were circumstance. Maybe the difference between you and them isn’t character. Maybe it’s circumstance. Every judgment is really a judgment of a story you’ve never heard. Maybe the biggest illusion is believing our own lives weren't shaped by circumstances too. Perhaps the veil we never lift is believing we got here entirely on our own.
